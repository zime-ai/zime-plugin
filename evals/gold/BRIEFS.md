# Gold-label briefs

Handoff for Tier 3 (see `EVALS.md`). Briefs are deliberately written by
someone who has **not** read the target skill's `references/` — so the
resulting fixtures aren't contaminated by knowing what the skill's output
format hunts for. Give a brief to whoever authors the fixture; they write
the meeting context and past-call transcript, and separately hand-label
what a great prep note for that meeting must contain.

## Brief 1: prep-note — the messy mid-deal handoff

Situation: a third call on a mid-market deal where the AE who ran the
first two calls just left the company, and a new AE plus an SE are walking
in. Author, as separate files:

- A meeting-context block for the upcoming call (use the field list in
  `skills/prep-note/SKILL.md`, synthetic names only).
- Transcripts of the two prior calls. By construction they must contain:
  - one commitment the vendor side made and has NOT delivered (the gold
    label: a great prep note surfaces this first);
  - one attendee on the upcoming invite who has never appeared on a prior
    call, from a different company domain (partner or consultant);
  - one competitor mentioned in passing with a specific historical reason
    they were rejected — not a current threat, but a landmine if the new
    AE reopens it naively;
  - one pain the prospect raised and explicitly downplayed (separates a
    prep note that weighs evidence from one that lists everything).
- A hand-written gold label: the 5-8 facts a prep note for this meeting
  must contain, and 2-3 facts it must NOT contain (plausible-sounding
  things the sources don't actually support — the invention trap).

Score recall against the must-contain list and precision against the
invention trap. Format compliance (seven sections, gaps marked, links) is
Tier 2's job, not this brief's.
